0 · Foreword and slop warning

Fox Tail is an alternative firmware for the Tap-O-Matic module by Harry Richardson, which is in turn based on the OAM Time Machine. It is therefore also open source and the code can be found here. It is largely based on the harmonic engine from Arturia's Pigments: Cluster and Shepard shapers will look familiar to those who have used it.

This project is primarily personal. It is genuine slop in several ways. That it's largely vibe coded. That many of the design decisions are tailored to personal needs rather than conventions or common sense. And that the quality control is decidedly suboptimal. You might see imperfections if you scrutinize the output with a scope. Relatedly, there are probably claims in this manual that are not completely true.

That said, I hope you find it useful, so I've put effort into making it somewhat presentable, and I'm open to suggestions. I've zero prior experience designing Eurorack firmware, so it's possible that I'd missed something super obvious!

The following manual is mostly AI generated.

1 · How it makes sound

Fox Tail is a stereo additive oscillator. Ninety-six sine oscillators run in parallel. The panel is a set of tools for choosing which of them sound, how loud, at what frequency ratio, and where in the stereo fieldPartial k normally sits at k × f0 — partial 1 is the fundamental, partial 2 the octave, and so on. Every control on the panel does exactly one of four things:

What it changes Controls
Which partials sound, and how loud the eight band sliders, the FADE TILT pots, SHIFT, BRIGHT/DARK, ODD ONLY
Where the partials sit in frequency COARSE, V/OCT, FINE, INHARM, and the shaper (WIN START, WIN SIZE, PHI/CLUSTERS, DENSITY)
Where they sit in the stereo field STEREO
Nothing you can hear directly the two jacks marked UNUSED — Fox Tail never reads them

The bank is divided into eight bands

The eight sliders to the right of INHARM each own a slice of the harmonic series. The slices are geometric — equal width in octaves, not in harmonics — and they tile the bank exactly, so the leftmost slider owns the fundamental alone and the rightmost owns forty-three partials. The panel prints where each one starts: ×1 ×2 ×4 ×6 ×10 ×18 ×31 ×54.

Slider ×1 ×2 ×4 ×6 ×10 ×18 ×31 ×54
Partials 1 2–3 4–5 6–9 10–17 18–30 31–53 54–96

That the first slider is the fundamental on its own is worth remembering: it is the one control that is exactly one partial. There is, however, a couple of scenarios where it controls neighboring partials as well.

2 · The band sliders — level and fill

Each of sliders ×1…×54 does two jobs at once, split across its travel:

  • All the way up from zero: the band's level rises.
  • Above the halfway point, additionally: the band fills in — more of its partials start sounding, one at a time, spreading outward from a seed partial.

So all eight sliders parked at half gives you a skeleton comb: exactly eight partials, one per band, each at its band's full level. Push a slider past half and its band thickens up into a full cluster of harmonics. The coupling is deliberate — a band with more partials in it genuinely is louder, so level and count move together and the drawn envelope stays believable at any setting.

The seed each band grows from is always a real partial, never a point between two, which is what makes “one partial at full level” exact rather than approximate — and what makes SHIFT's center the position where every seed has a partial sitting on it.

Practical: to draw a formant-ish hump, set the sliders in an arc and leave the bands you want thin at half. To play “additive organ”, run everything at half and use SHIFT (below) to slide the eight chosen harmonics up and down the series.

Each slider also has its own CV jack in the top row. The jack is summed with the slider and clamped, so a slider parked at zero is opened by CV, and one parked at the top ignores it.

3 · FADE TILT — where each band fills from

The six pots labeled under the FADE TILT (HP-BP-LP) bracket set the fill order for bands ×4 through ×54. The first two bands have no pot: ×1 is the fundamental on its own, so there is no order to choose, and ×2 (partials 2 and 3) is pinned to fill upward, so the bottom of the spectrum always arrives lowest partial first.

Pot position Fill order Sounds like
Full CCW starts at the band's highest partial and grows downward highpass — the band's top edge survives
center starts in the middle and grows both ways bandpass
Full CW starts at the band's lowest partial and grows upward lowpass

Distance is counted in partials, not in octaves, so a pot means the same thing in a band holding two of them and one holding twenty-three.

Highpass sits at CCW so that building a spectral hump is one diagonal gesture: a band to the left of your peak wants to keep the partials closest to the peak — which are its high ones — so the pots rise left-to-right exactly as the sliders do.

4 · INHARM — the stiff string

Slider 1 (with its own CV jack) bends the whole series away from whole-number ratios, using the stiff-string law fk = k·f0·√(1+B·k²). Because the stretch goes as k², the low partials stay nearly pure while the top of the bank runs sharp — piano-ish at low settings, bell and gong metallic at the top. At full travel the upper partials land several times above their harmonic positions.

Why it matters: this is the one thing a subtractive voice fundamentally cannot fake. A filter can shape a harmonic spectrum; it cannot make it inharmonic.

5 · SHIFT and FINE

SHIFT — slides the drawn shape along the series

SHIFT multiplies the coordinate that both the band envelope and the fill comb are read at, ±1 octave from center. No partial changes frequency. Only which partials are picked, and how loud they are. Turned clockwise the shape moves up the series, counter-clockwise down.

center is genuinely the home position, not just the middle of the travel: it is the one place where every band's seed lands exactly on a partial, so a skeleton comb is eight partials at full level. Move away from center and the comb slides between partials — seeds drift off their harmonics and the chosen ones fade in and out. That is the gesture, not a fault, but it does mean a band with its slider below half can go quiet at the extremes of the pot.

Paired with the skeleton comb (all sliders at half) this is the single most musical gesture on the panel: eight chosen harmonics scanning up and down the series over a pitch that never moves.

FINE — ±1 semitone

COARSE covers 4 octaves in one sweep, which makes it hard to tune by hand. FINE adds ±1 semitone, center = in tune, clockwise = sharp. The firmware snaps a small deadzone at noon, so it's easier to dial in.

6 · COARSE and V/OCT

COARSE sets the fundamental over 12.5 Hz – 200 Hz, exponentially — four octaves, so the knob's center lands on the geometric mean (50 Hz), not the arithmetic one. The range is deliberately low: this is the root of an additive bank, and the partials, not the root, carry the top end.

The V/O jack is a calibrated 1 V/octave input, summed with COARSE and FINE. On a calibrated unit tracking holds within a few cents over two octaves. There is no upper clamp on the resulting fundamental — past Nyquist the partials fade out on their own, which tracks honestly instead of parking the note inaudibly high. The floor is 0.001 Hz, which is to say there isn't one either: a negative pitch CV takes the whole bank down to LFO rates, and −5 V (the input's electrical limit) puts f0 at 0.39 Hz.

Nyquist is real. At 48 kHz and f0 = 220 Hz the whole 96-partial bank fits under Nyquist; at 880 Hz only 27 partials do, and the top five sliders have nothing left to play. Partials fade out over the top 5% of the range rather than switching off, and a band whose partials have all run past Nyquist pulses its LED dimly rather than just going dark.

7 · BRIGHT/DARK and ODD ONLY

The tilt switch (right-hand switch)

The underlying slope of the untouched spectrum:

Position Law Character
DARK (down) 1/r a sawtooth's −6 dB/octave; roughly what an acoustic source does
BRIGHT (up) 1/√r equal power per octave; ~3 dB/octave brighter, ≈20 dB over the bank

BRIGHT makes every band slider equally effective, which is a lovely panel property, but it leaves the top octaves loud enough that you hear individual partials beating. DARK is the safer bed for drones.

The flip is level-compensated, but not perfectly: BRIGHT sits a few dB quieter than DARK, on purpose — it has more loud partials stacking up, so it is given more room to peak into. Expect the switch to change the loudness a little as well as the color. (Why, and by how much: appendix A.)

ODD ONLY (gate jack)

A gate high mutes the even-numbered partials, leaving 1, 3, 5, … — hollow, clarinet-ish. Unpatched reads low, which is ALL partials, so the jack costs nothing left empty. It is a digital input: gates and squares, not CV. Under ODD ONLY, SHEPARD's glide takes twice as long to come round (see below).

The switch between the two spectra is smoothed rather than instant, so it reads as a timbral move rather than a click — which makes this a cool rhythmic control. A gate sequence, a manual trigger button, or a clock divider on this jack gives you a hollow/full alternation in time with the patch, over a drone that is otherwise completely static. See the recipes.

8 · The shaper window — WIN START and WIN SIZE

The two big knobs marked WIN START and WIN SIZE define a movable window over the bank. Partials inside the window get retuned; partials outside stay exactly where they are. This is what lets the weird stuff happen without losing the pitch — leave the fundamental outside the window and it anchors the note while everything above it goes strange. There's LED feedback when you adjust the window, showing you roughly where it lands relative to the bands.

Both knobs are exponential over 1…96 partials, so the low end of the bank gets most of the travel. Both are CV-able (START and SIZE jacks), which makes the window itself a playable target. They mean the same thing in both CLUSTER and SHEPARD modes.

In SHEPARD the window start and size snap to whole partials (two at a time under ODD ONLY). That is not cosmetic: it puts every partial's window weight at exactly 0 or 1, which is what makes the endless-glide wrap seamless. It also makes “exclude the fundamental” a repeatable gesture instead of a hunt. In CLUSTER the window is continuous.

9 · CLUSTER vs SHEPARD

The left-hand switch picks what happens inside the window. The two mode knobs — PHI / CLUSTERS and WIN DUCK / DENSITY — are the only two controls that change meaning, which is why each carries two names on the panel: PHI and WIN DUCK in SHEPARD, CLUSTERS and DENSITY in CLUSTER. Below, this manual uses whichever name the mode under discussion calls for.

CLUSTER (switch down) SHEPARD (switch up)
PHI / CLUSTERS partials per cluster; center = 1, nothing shifts. CCW collapses onto the cluster's low end, CW onto its high end glissando rate; center = frozen, CW = rising, CCW = falling
WIN DUCK / DENSITY density: how far the collapse goes, 0 at CCW to 0.995 at CW ducks the windowed partials; CCW = unity gain, CW = window silent
Envelope read at the partial index — a slider always owns the same harmonics however far the collapse dragged them the shifted frequency — partials slide through the drawn envelope
Sliders act as a chooser of ingredients an EQ on the output
PHI/CLUSTERS is center-neutral in both modes, with a software deadzone at noon. A stock build has no mechanical detent, so trust the sound, not the pointer: at noon nothing shifts and nothing glides.

CLUSTER

The windowed partials are grouped into clusters of m partials each, and every member slides toward one end of its own cluster. Density sets how far it gets.

  • DENSITY at 0 (fully CCW): nothing moves. There is a 2% deadzone at the bottom so a pot that doesn't quite reach its end can't leave a detune behind.
  • Density 0.9–1.0: the useful zone. Members land within a hair of each other and beat — thick chorus, then slow shimmer, then near-unison. Beat periods run into seconds.
  • CLUSTERS CCW (collapse down): the survivors land on exact multiples of m — a comb aligned to f0, so it thickens the note. This is the loud side, and the one the gain compensation is working hardest on.
  • CLUSTERS CW (collapse up): the same spacing, offset by m−1, so it is not aligned to f0 — it rings and adds a second implied pitch instead of thickening. This side gets quieter as you push it, and at the extreme (one cluster, full density) the whole bank piles onto the window's top partial, where the tilt has least to give. That is the physics, not a compensation artefact; if it goes silent see troubleshooting.
  • CLUSTERS reaches far enough that the whole window becomes a single cluster.
  • Density near 1/4, 1/2 and 1 lands on spectra that imply a subharmonic (with m = 2, d = 0.5 you get 1, 1.5, 3, 3.5 … = multiples of f0/2).
Cluster is the loud mode, and it is compensated for you — see Levels & gain compensation. If you build a version with the compensation defeated (NO_NORM=1), the density knob swings the level by many dB.

SHEPARD

Based on Shepard Tone, except our sines are in harmonic series rather than octaves apart. Every partial in the window shifts toward the next one up: rk = k + φ. At φ = 1 the whole ensemble is identical to φ = 0 with the partials relabeled — so a continuously rising φ produces a glide that never restarts. Partial 1 travels a whole octave per wrap and partial 72 moves 24 cents, so the low partials sweep enormously and the high ones barely move; what earns the name is the wrap, not uniform motion.

  • PHI sets the rate, exponential from 0.01 to 10 partials/second, center frozen, CW rising. The illusion holds to roughly half a wrap per second — the usable range is the bottom of the knob. The top of the travel is a zap effect, deliberately.
  • The CLUSTERS jack is the better driver. Patch a slow ramp LFO into it. The firmware reads the jack's change, not its level, and folds any reset larger than half a wrap back into continuous motion — so the LFO's reset is not a click, and its depth does not have to be tuned to exactly one partial.
  • The ends fade. The bank is finite, so a shift leaves nothing at the bottom and pushes one off the top. Both ends fade to silence over four partials, keyed to the window rather than the bank, which is what makes the wrap exact. The cost is a narrow notch just above the window's bottom edge — static, a gap rather than a loop.
  • ODD ONLY doubles the cycle length: the odd set only lands back on itself after two index steps, so a wrap takes twice as long at the same apparent glide speed.
  • WIN DUCK fades the window out, inverted so the knob at rest (CCW) is unity gain. Sweeping it up fades out just the moving partials and leaves the anchored ones playing.
  • The band edges soften while it glides. Partials slide through the drawn envelope, so a hard edge at a band boundary would tick once per crossing; SHEPARD crossfades both the band's level and its fill across the boundary to stop that. The crossfade only exists while φ is actually moving — park the glide and the bands go hard-edged again, exactly like CLUSTER, so a slider that is down means down. Expect a slight swell at the band edges as a glide starts.

10 · STEREO

The STEREO pot is not a width fader. It morphs between three stereo configurations and then spends its last quarter on motion rather than shape. The partials are distributed across eight pan slots by a fixed pattern that throws neighboring partials as far apart as possible, so what you hear is a cloud rather than eight discrete positions.

What each stage sounds like

Pot Behavior
0 – 8% a genuine mono park. Nothing is panned; use it when the patch has to fold down.
8 – 50% morph from mono into SCATTER — a static, even fan of 8 positions. Neighboring partials sit far apart, so a dense band smears into a wide sheet.
50 – 75% morph from SCATTER into ORBIT, with rotation creeping up from a standstill. Around the middle of this range you get a slow, barely-moving image.
75 – 100% the shape is fixed; the travel now buys motion. Rotation goes 0.04 → 0.20 revolutions/second, and the odd slot subset counter-rotates so the two clouds cross about twice per revolution.

Four things worth knowing

  • The fundamental is always centered. Spread is scaled by ratio − 1, so partial 1 sits dead center and the image opens up above it. A fully spread fundamental smears the whole thing; anchoring it at the bottom is what makes wide settings still read as one note.
  • Nothing reaches a hard pan except SCATTER's two end slots. ORBIT's radius stops at 0.8 on purpose: hard panning puts half the bank in one channel at full gain instead of all of it at 0.707, which is the only thing here that ever moved a peak.
  • ODD ONLY does not shove the image sideways. Each configuration is built so the odd and even partial subsets stay balanced around center — that property is asserted by a test, not hoped for.
  • In SHEPARD the image moves on its own. A partial carries its pan slot with it as it climbs, and each time the glide wraps, every slot hands over to the next — so the field keeps turning even with STEREO parked well down in the SCATTER range, where the pot itself asks for no motion at all. Measured at STEREO around a third of travel, a single partial swings across two thirds of the field on its own. If you want a still image in SHEPARD, you have to go to MONO.
Panel note: OUT LEFT and OUT RIGHT are correct as printed. Fox Tail is stereo out only; the pair of jacks marked UNUSED are the delay firmware's audio inputs, and this firmware never reads them — doing so is against the module's noise budget.

11 · LEDs

  • LED 1 follows INHARM.
  • LEDs 2–9 normally show band level. A band whose slider is up but whose partials have all passed Nyquist pulses dimly — “turned down” and “nothing left to play” must not look alike.
  • Touch WIN START or WIN SIZE and the row flips to a window peek — the lit LEDs show where the shaper window sits over the bank, with its edges partly lit — until 1.5 s after you stop moving the knob.

12 · CV, calibration and limits

The jacks

Jack Goes to Notes
Top row × 9 INHARM + the eight band sliders summed with the slider, then clamped
V/O pitch calibrated 1 V/oct; summed with COARSE and FINE
START / SIZE window start / width summed with the knob
CLUSTERS the PHI/CLUSTERS knob the exception: CLUSTER sums it with the knob; SHEPARD takes the rate from the knob and moves φ by this jack's change
DENSITY the WIN DUCK/DENSITY knob summed with the knob
ODD ONLY parity gate input, digital: high = odd partials only

Calibration

Three steps, one stored record. Power up with INHARM fully up + left switch up + right switch down, then flip the right switch to capture each step:

  1. 1 V patched to V/OCT
  2. 3 V patched to V/OCT
  3. everything unpatched — this captures the CV jack nulls

Step 3 matters as much as the pitch fit: the summing jacks idle around −0.02 rather than 0, and since the firmware adds jack to knob and clamps, that bias ate the top 2.5% of every affected control's travel — which is why an uncalibrated unit can't quite reach a single cluster at full CW.

Bench-testing gotcha: on USB power alone the Daisy's analog domain is dead — CV inputs rail at ~0.99 and audio is silent, while the pots and sliders keep working normally. Always test CV and audio with rack power connected (USB may stay attached for the serial log).

Limits

  • 96 partials, always running. No upper clamp on f0 — the Nyquist fade retires the bank instead — and a floor of 0.001 Hz, which a negative pitch CV can reach.
  • At 48 kHz, partials above Nyquist fade out over the top 5% of the range.
  • Phases are randomized at power-up and never reset — the module has no “retrigger”.

13 · Patch recipes

Start here — one sine

The simplest sound the module makes, and the best way to hear what everything else is doing to it.

  • Slider ×1 up; every other band slider down. That band is the fundamental on its own, so what you get is a single sine — no fill, no cluster, nothing to go wrong.
  • SHIFT at center. This is the one that matters. SHIFT slides the whole chosen shape along the harmonic series, so off center the ×1 band stops sitting on partial 1 — turn it a few hours clockwise and partial 2 joins in underneath at half level, which is no longer a sine.
  • INHARM down, CLUSTERS at center, DENSITY fully CCW.
  • Left switch down (CLUSTER).

Use it to check tuning and V/OCT tracking, or as the thing you build on: bring up ×2, then ×4, and listen to the note thicken one band at a time.

In SHEPARD it needs one more move. Nudge WIN START up off its CCW end — about a tenth of the travel puts the window's start at partial 2. Left fully CCW, the fundamental is inside the window, where the glide shifts it off pitch and the end fade takes it to near silence. Outside the window it sits at exactly 1×f0 at full level, the same sine as CLUSTER gives. That is the general rule in one gesture: whatever you leave outside the window keeps the note.

The plain tone — everything switched off

The other patch worth being able to find blindfolded: the full harmonic series with the shaper doing nothing at all. This is what the sliders draw on.

  • Left switch down (CLUSTER), CLUSTERS at center, DENSITY fully CCW.
  • All band sliders wherever you want the spectrum; SHIFT at center; INHARM down.

Either of those two knobs at rest switches the other one off. The collapse the mode applies is “how many partials per cluster” multiplied by “how far they travel”, so a zero in either term is a zero. CLUSTERS at center means one partial per cluster — a cluster with nothing to collapse toward — and DENSITY fully CCW means nothing travels. Turn one up and you will hear nothing until the other leaves its rest position; that is not a broken knob, it is the pair needing each other.

ODD ONLY as a rhythm part

ODD ONLY is a jack, not a switch, and it is the only control on the panel that rewards being played rather than set. Muting the even partials moves the tone between a full harmonic series and a hollow, clarinet-like one, and the engine slews between the two rather than stepping — so a gate does not click.

  • Build any drone you like — the plain tone above is a good start, and the effect is strongest with the low and middle bands up.
  • Patch a gate sequencer, a clock divider, or a manual trigger button into the ODD ONLY jack. Half-time or quarter-time against the rest of the patch works better than every beat: the change is timbral, and it needs room to be heard.
  • A momentary button is worth a channel on its own here. It is the one gesture that makes an otherwise motionless drone feel performed.

In SHEPARD it also doubles the length of the glide cycle, so gating it while a glissando runs stretches and shrinks the loop as well as thinning the tone.

The endless glissando

  • Left switch up (SHEPARD)
  • WIN START fully CCW — window starts at partial 1, so everything moves
  • WIN SIZE fully CW — window covers the whole bank
  • WIN DUCK fully CCW — unity gain
  • All band sliders up. A drawn hump does not work here: the sliders control fill as well as level, so pulling the top band down thins it to one partial instead of fading it. The engine fades the ends for you.
  • PHI just off center. The knob is its own ramp — it sets a glissando rate and the module runs it for you, no LFO needed. The usable range is the very bottom of the travel: the illusion holds to about half a wrap per second, and above that you hear the individual sweeps and it turns into a zap effect.

Drive it from the knob, not from a cable. The CLUSTERS jack does move φ — it reads the jack's change rather than its level, which is good for hand-shaped or one-shot sweeps — but an external ramp does not hold the endless illusion in practice. Either tilt works; ODD ONLY doubles the cycle length.

Skeleton comb

All band sliders at half, SHIFT at center: eight partials, one per band, each at full level — an eight-tooth comb across the whole series, and the clearest way to hear what the bands are. ×1 is always the fundamental and ×2 always partial 2; the other six are placed by the FADE TILT pots, CCW = the band's highest partial, CW = its lowest.

Keep the pots at one end or the other for a clean comb. Mid-travel the seed lands between two partials and the pair shares the level, which sweeps smoothly — musical when you want to scan a band, but it is two quiet partials rather than one loud one.

Half travel on the sliders is a knife edge, and that is the point of the design rather than a flaw: a couple of percent above it and each band starts pulling in neighbors, so eight partials becomes seventeen. Ride the sliders just under half for the pure comb, just over for a comb that is starting to thicken.

Now sweep SHIFT. It does more than slide the comb along the series: turned clockwise it packs the comb tighter, so extra harmonics crowd in around each tooth and eight partials grows toward twenty; turned counter-clockwise it stretches the comb until some bands have no partial near their seed at all and drop out, down to six. Center is the clean eight, one end is a denser spectrum, the other is a sparser one — all without a single partial changing pitch.

Metallic body, stable pitch

Left switch down (CLUSTER). Set WIN START above the fundamental so partials 1–3 stay anchored; WIN SIZE wide. CLUSTERS a little CW of center (small clusters), DENSITY up into the 0.9+ zone. Add INHARM to taste. The anchored low partials hold the note while everything above beats and rings.

Appendix — under the hood

None of this is needed to play the module. It is here because the numbers are real and measured, and because knowing where the headroom went explains why Fox Tail is quiet.

A · Levels and gain compensation

Fox Tail runs a fixed headroom budget — the Hammond drawbar answer — rather than dynamic normalization. Nothing ducks when you push a slider up. Three fixed pieces of gain staging keep that honest:

1 · Headroom

A constant 0.356 sits in every band gain, sized against the worst case of all eight sliders at maximum. Because the partials start at random phases, a bank sums as √N — so an ordinary two- or three-band patch sits well below the case being protected. All sliders up lands at −13.0 dBFS RMS on the DARK slope (0.472 peak); the skeleton comb, all sliders at half, is −20.0 dBFS. Fox Tail is a quiet module by design; expect to gain it up downstream.

2 · Tilt compensation

Summed over the bank, equal power per octave is about 5 dB louder than a sawtooth slope, so the BRIGHT/DARK switch is compensated. It is matched at the peak, not at the ear: all sliders up measures 0.472 peak on DARK against 0.457 on BRIGHT, while BRIGHT is about 3 dB lower in RMS. That is on purpose. DARK has the lower crest factor, so it has headroom BRIGHT cannot use, and the extra is spent where the low end is rather than left on the table.

3 · Cluster compensation

This is the big one. In CLUSTER the tilt and the band envelope are read at the shifted frequency, so collapsing the bank down-spectrum genuinely adds power — about 18 dB at full density with the window over the whole bank. An attenuation derived from the geometry alone (density, cluster size, window) is folded into the band gains, holding the level within 2.2 dB across the density knob.

  • It only ever attenuates. Collapsing up (CLUSTERS CW) gets quieter on its own, and the model would ask for gain there — that request is refused, because it would also reach partials the window never moved.
  • It is computed from the control positions alone, never from the audio. That is not a style choice: gains derived from the engine's own amplitude state produced audible ~8 kHz artifacts on hardware, twice.
  • It assumes a flat envelope, so with only a few sliders up it over-corrects a little, and at the very bottom of WIN START at full density it over-cuts by about 4.6 dB. That corner is the loud one, so erring quiet there is cheap.
  • Exactly 0.00 dB at the CLUSTERS center detent — with no collapse asked for, nothing is taken.

4 · The output clip

Linear below 0.85, parabolic to a hard cap at 1.15. As shipped nothing reaches the knee — an oscillator patch passes through bit-exact — so the clip is insurance for Cluster's beat transients, not a stage in the sound. Worst case measured over 22,875 patch combinations: peak 0.833, i.e. 0.17 dB of margin. That is deliberately thin: the budget is spent, and any change that makes a patch louder trips the guard rather than quietly eating the margin.
